# Power Saving & Sleep Mode

Since the WiCAN Pro remains plugged into your vehicle's OBD2 port 24/7, managing power consumption is critical. The **Power Saving** tab allows you to configure exactly when the device should power down to protect your car's battery, and when it should wake up to report data.

## 1. Core Sleep Configuration

The WiCAN Pro determines if your vehicle is running or parked by monitoring the 12V system voltage. When the alternator is spinning, the voltage is high (usually 13.5V+). When the engine is off, the voltage drops to the resting battery voltage (usually ~12.6V).

| Setting | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Sleep Mode** | Master toggle to enable or disable the power-saving engine. |
| **Sleep Voltage** | The threshold that triggers the sleep timer (Range: 12.0V - 15.0V). |
| **Sleep After** | How long the voltage must remain below the threshold before the device actually powers down (Range: 1 - 30 mins). |


### How the Sleep Logic Actually Works
Behind the scenes, the firmware operates on a strict state machine to prevent "bouncing" (falling asleep and waking up rapidly if the voltage fluctuates):

1. **Voltage Drops:** When the battery voltage drops below your **Sleep Voltage** setting, the WiCAN enters a "Low Voltage" warning state and starts the **Sleep After** countdown timer.
2. **Timer Expires:** If the voltage stays low for the entire duration of the timer, the WiCAN gracefully shuts down the CAN transceiver, drops the Wi-Fi/VPN connections, and enters deep sleep.
3. **The Wakeup Trigger:** The device monitors the voltage even while sleeping. If the voltage spikes by at least `+0.1V` above your Sleep Voltage setting (indicating the engine or EV contactors have started), it enters a "Wake Pending" state.
4. **Resuming Operation:** Once the voltage is stable for 1-2 seconds, the WiCAN performs a fresh system reboot to ensure a clean start and resumes standard operation.

> [!WARNING]
> **Disabling Sleep Mode** will keep the Wi-Fi, CAN transceiver, and main processor running indefinitely. This will continuously drain your vehicle's battery and should only be used temporarily for testing or diagnostics.

## 2. Background Polling

Even when your vehicle is parked and the WiCAN is asleep, you may still want to check in on the battery health or other vehicle info like tire pressure. You can configure the WiCAN to periodically wake up, connect to Wi-Fi, run its Automate groups, and go back to sleep. For Vehicle Group, since the car is most likely off, not running, you want the Vehicle Group set to Always.

* **Disable Wakeups:** The device will only wake up when you physically start the vehicle.
* **Periodic Interval:** Wakes the device up every `X` minutes (e.g., every 60 minutes) for a brief data sync.
* **Scheduled (Time of Day):** Wakes the device up at specific times. You can add multiple triggers (e.g., `08:00 AM` and `08:00 PM`). 

> [!NOTE]
> Scheduled wakeups rely on the **Local Timezone** setting. You must ensure your timezone is configured correctly in the **System** tab for this feature to trigger at the correct local time.
